## Task 4 — Time and geometry helpers [done]

- **`src/domain/time.ts`** (new) holds the seven responsibilities the plan lists, all pure functions of numbers, so the grid, the editors, and the progress sentences cannot disagree about what a number means. Exported: `DAY_SPAN_MINUTES` (= 960, derived from `DAY_END_MINUTE - DAY_START_MINUTE`, never re-typed), a `MinuteRange` interface (`startMinute`/`endMinute`, half-open), `formatClock`, `formatDuration`, `snapDragMinute`, `isOnTypedStep`, `isWithinDay`, `rangesOverlap`, `topPercent`, `heightPercent`. Constants are imported from `@/domain/types` rather than re-derived, so task 3's pinned numbers stay the single source. No `Date` anywhere, no `any`.
- **Clock text** is 24-hour with no leading zero on the hour and always two digits on the minutes: `8:00`, `9:30`, `17:00`, and the day edges `6:00`/`22:00`. A loop over all 1441 minutes of a day asserts the shape `^\d{1,2}:\d{2}$`, so no minute can print as `8:5` or `08:00`.
- **Duration text** drops the minutes on a whole hour and the hours before one, so a 300-minute quota reads `5 hours` and a 100-minute session reads `1 hour 40 minutes` rather than a decimal. The four documented strings are asserted verbatim, plus the 59/60 boundary, `1 minute` vs `2 minutes`, `1 hour 1 minute`, and `0 minutes` for absent travel (a length, not a silence). A loop over 0–720 asserts the text never contains `60 minutes`, which is the one wrong-looking thing a naive `Math.floor` split can produce.
- **Drag snap** is the nearest quarter hour. The tie the plan asked to be documented cannot actually happen: a 15-minute step would have to cut a whole minute in half to reach one, and 7.5 minutes past is not a time the board can hold. The test says so in a comment and then pins the real behaviour from both sides — 6:07 is 7 minutes past and 8 short, so it snaps back to `6:00`; 6:08 flips the distance and snaps forward to `6:15`. A loop over the whole day asserts every result is on the step, is idempotent, and moves no more than half a step (7.5 minutes, never reached).
- **Typed step** is five minutes: `isOnTypedStep(100)` for 1:40 is true, 1:37 is false, and 6:00/22:00 are both fine. A field that misses the step is refused by the caller, not snapped — `snapDragMinute(100)` is 105, which is exactly why the two rules stay separate (asserted in a closing test that the steps really are 15 and 5).
- **Bounds** are day edges only: `isWithinDay({360, 1320})` accepts the whole visible day (so a block may start at 6:00 and end at 22:00), `{720, 840}` accepts a block in the 12:00–14:00 hole, and 5:55 / 22:05 are refused. The function deliberately says nothing about length — the minimum-length rules belong to the mutations that return `too-short`.
- **Overlap** is `a.start < b.end && b.start < a.end`, so touching stretches are allowed (8:00–12:00 against 12:00–14:00, and a commute ending exactly at the work start) while any shared minute, including full containment, is refused. One test case caught a real mistake while writing it: I had claimed 700–800 was clear of 480–720, and the assertion failed correctly. Fixed the fixture to 14:10–15:00, which is genuinely free.
- **Geometry** is expressed as the `GridBlockView` field names the plan defines: `topPercent(minute)` puts 6:00 at 0, 12:00 at 37.5, and 22:00 at 100, and `heightPercent(minutes)` makes a 15-minute block `(15 / 960) * 100` and a 100-minute block `(100 / 960) * 100`. The 960 lives inside this module, so the offset from 6:00 cannot be forgotten at a call site the way a bare subtraction could.

## Task 6 — Palette [done]

- **`src/colors/palette.ts`** (new) is the whole color surface: `COLOR_IDS`, the `ColorClasses` interface, `PALETTE`, and `nextColorId`. The task also says to "add the twelve `ColorId` values", but task 3 already fixed the union in `domain/types.ts`; the union is not duplicated here, so `PALETTE` is a total map over one source of truth.
- **Three roles per hue, one shade each: the activity block is the 600 step, its travel strip is the 200 step, the label is the 900 step.** The 600 step is one darker than the `bg-sky-500` this plan offers as its example, and that deviation is deliberate and measured, not a preference. I converted every hue's oklch values from the installed `tailwindcss/theme.css` to sRGB and computed WCAG contrast: white label text on the 500 step is under 4.5:1 for **seven** of the twelve hues (amber 3.3:1, lime 3.5:1, orange 3.8:1, rose and green 4.0:1, red 4.3:1, pink 4.46:1), and the block's name is the one signal the product scope insists must not be color-only. At the 600 step the worst hue, orange, is 4.6:1 and the rest are darker; `text-<hue>-900` on `bg-<hue>-200` is 8.1:1 at worst, so travel can stay a lighter shade of the same color and still be read. The numbers are in the module comment so this is not relitigated later.
- **White on the solid shade is not a map value.** It is the same for all twelve, so repeating it twelve times would be noise; the map's `text` field is the readable label color for the *soft* shade. ## Task 7 — Footprints and placement checks [done]

- **`src/domain/schedule.ts`** (new) answers two questions and owns the vocabulary both need: which minutes of a day are held, and whether a proposed block may take them. Exported: the types `PlacedTarget`, `BlockTarget`, `TravelDirection`, `OccupiedStretch`, `Footprint`; the builders `workFootprint`, `commuteFootprint`, `sessionFootprint`, `oneOffFootprint`, `footprintRange`; the occupancy readers `placedOccupancy`, `dayOccupancy`, `freeStretches`; and the three checks `checkFootprintFits`, `checkCommute`, `checkDayPlan`. Nothing here mutates a board: task 8's work mutations, task 10's session mutations, and task 12's grid all ask these questions instead of re-deriving a comparison, which is the whole point of the module.
- **One type carries the idea: `OccupiedStretch` is a union on `role`.** Work carries a `target`; a commute carries a `direction` and *no* target, because a commute hangs off the day rather than off a block; an activity carries a target; a travel stretch carries both. So the illegal combinations do not type-check rather than being documented and hoped for. Proved with four throwaway probe files compiled by `tsc -b --force`: a commute with a target fails with TS2353, a travel without a direction fails with TS2322, an activity with a direction fails with TS2353, and a travel belonging to a work interval fails with TS2322. All probes deleted.
- **`Footprint` is just `OccupiedStretch[]`, and footprints are contiguous.** Travel before, then the activity, then travel after, with no gap inside, so `footprintRange` is the stretch that has to fit inside 6:00–22:00 and inside one free gap. The rule is then stated once, the way the product states it: a session, travel included, must sit entirely inside one free stretch. Zero travel emits *nothing* rather than a zero-length stretch, so absent commute and absent travel read the same way.
- **`Session.startMinute` is the first minute the block occupies, not the start of its activity.** This is the one convention this task had to decide and it changes what every later task writes, so it is stated in the module header and pinned by a test: a session with 20 minutes of travel before and 45 of activity starting at 12:30 runs 12:30–13:35, and `activityMinutes` is the middle stretch. It is what lets a tap on the grid mean "the block starts here" and lets travel be drawn *on* the block instead of beside it. Worth knowing before task 17's editors, since a typed "start" field will be the block's left edge, not its first activity minute.
- **`checkFootprintFits` owns the rules a placement cannot be judged without:** a real length per stretch, travel and commute at least five minutes or nothing, the whole footprint inside the visible day, and inside one free stretch of that day. It deliberately does **not** check the minimum activity or work length: those differ (`MIN_ACTIVITY_MINUTES` 15 against `MIN_WORK_MINUTES` 15 today, but they are different rules with different owners) and belong to the mutation that knows which kind of block it is placing. That is task 8's and task 10's business, and there is a test saying so, so the gap is a decision on the record rather than an oversight.
- **`checkCommute` exists because it is the one rule occupancy cannot see.** A day with no work intervals has no commute footprint at all — there is nothing for the minutes to attach to — so a check that only looked at stretches would happily accept a commute that occupies no particular time. `commuteFootprint` returns an empty list there rather than inventing a position, and `checkCommute` turns setting one into `commute-without-work`. Both halves are tested, including that it answers both directions together, since a day off is a day off.
- **`checkDayPlan(board, day, plan)` is task 8's workhorse, and one decision in it matters.** Work is compared against work *inside the proposed plan*, never against the day already stored, because the day being changed is not what the board holds yet. That is exactly what makes "appointment 8:00–10:00, so work is 10:00–12:00 and 14:00–17:00" expressible as one replacement plan instead of a series of moves, and what lets a single 10:00–15:00 interval replace the two preset ones. Its refusals come back in the order a person would want to hear them: the commute has nowhere to attach, then a work interval that is too short, then two intervals crossing, then a proposed stretch leaving the day or landing on a placed block. It also bounds-checks the commute, which is the "start earlier than 8:00, but only if the commute still fits" rule from the product scope.
- **A real bug, found by running the tests, worth recording.** Both `footprintRange` and `commuteFootprint` first seeded their min/max with `DAY_START_MINUTE`, so every computed start collapsed to 6:00 — a 7:30 commute came out at 5:30, and every placement check refused everything as `overlaps`. Nineteen of the twenty-one failures came from that one line. It is now seeded from the first stretch, and the empty-footprint case is handled explicitly rather than by accident. The other two failures were slips in my own test arithmetic, caught by the same run: I had computed a 30-minute commute against a 45-minute gap, and I had read a session's `startMinute` as its activity start in two expectations after having just written down the opposite convention. Fixing the test rather than the code was the right call in both cases, because the code matched the convention the module documents.
- **`src/domain/schedule.test.ts`** (new, 68 tests) covers all nine done-when cases and then the surrounding rules: the four product-scope work examples as executable checks (leave at 12:00; an 8:00–10:00 appointment; a 9:30–10:30 appointment producing three intervals and a 9:30–10:30 hole; a weekend gaining work), a commute change refused because a session is in the way, a commute reaching past 22:00, the preset weekday, a day off, and both calls leaving the board and the candidate plan byte-identical (`structuredClone` snapshots, as task 5 established).
- **The four occupancy readers are tested against each other, not just individually.** `dayOccupancy` is asserted to equal the union of commute, work, and placed blocks; `freeStretches` is asserted to join two touching stretches rather than leaving a zero-width gap; and a session with travel is shown splitting its gap into two shorter ones, then handed back whole when it is excluded. Commute stretches carry no target, so excluding a block never takes the commute with it — also tested, since that is the easy way to break a move.
- **Fourteen wrong implementations were probed, and all fourteen were caught.** Each was applied to the real file and run against the suite: comparing work against the stored day instead of inside the plan (3 failures); ignoring `except` (3); matching `except` by kind instead of by block id (1); checking only the activity range and ignoring travel (2); attaching the commute to the first and last *stored* interval instead of the earliest start and latest end (1); forgetting the commute inside `checkDayPlan` (3); skipping the commute bounds check (2); accepting a commute on a day with no work (2); emitting a zero-width gap between touching stretches (2); dropping the five-minute travel minimum (2); treating a touching end as an overlap (3); accepting an empty footprint (1); dropping the minimum work length (1); and emitting an empty travel stretch when the minutes are zero (3). Every probe was reverted and the tree re-checked.

## Task 8 — Work and commute mutations [done]

- **`src/domain/work.ts`** (new) is five exported mutations — `addWorkInterval`, `moveWorkInterval`, `resizeWorkInterval`, `removeWorkInterval`, `setCommute` — and one private `commit(board, day, candidate)` they all go through. That function is the design: build the day that *would* result, hand it to `checkDayPlan`, and only then make a board. A work change is a whole new day rather than a patch on the old one, because the commute hangs off the outer ends of the day's work; move the first start and the morning commute has to move with it, and no function here knows what a commute is. The commute moves because the *day* is re-checked, not because a rule was copied into four call sites. `removeWorkInterval` goes through the same door even though a removal can only free time, so it is the one change that can never be refused for a reason of its own.
- **Signatures.** `addWorkInterval(board, day, range, createId)` takes the whole range, because adding one is what a day editor does with a start and an end it was given. `moveWorkInterval(board, day, id, startMinute)` slides and keeps the length, because a drag reports a drop and the block keeps its own height. `resizeWorkInterval(board, day, id, edge, minute)` moves one end and leaves the other, which is the one mutation behind both a grid resize and the two typed fields — "Grid resize of a work interval uses the same work mutation as the editor" from the plan, and 1:40 is legal. All five take a `Board` and return `Result<Board>`.
- **Three decisions are recorded in the module header and pinned by tests.** (1) *Work does not change days*: the id must already be on the day being changed, so a drop on another column is refused with `missing-work` rather than silently pulling an interval off its day — which days have work is the day editor's question, and this keeps a move one mutation instead of a remove plus an add. (2) *Removing the last interval clears both commute directions*, which is a rule and not a repair: a commute hangs off work, so `checkDayPlan`'s `commute-without-work` would otherwise refuse the removal of the last interval and a day would be impossible to empty. (3) *The five-minute typed step is checked in the mutation, not in a component*, so a 1:37 start is told to use 1:35 or 1:40 rather than that it is outside 6:00–22:00, which is true and useless.
- **A commute is the one place the two minute rules are asked in the other order, and that is a real decision.** Every whole number under five is also off the five-minute step, so a person who typed three minutes of commute would be told their number is not a multiple of five. `commuteRefusal` asks the `MIN_TRAVEL_MINUTES` floor first, so three minutes is `too-short` and seven is `not-a-step`. Both refusals leave the same two choices, so the one that explains the rule is the one worth saying. A test failed on exactly this before the code was changed, which is the argument for having written it down first.
- **Two members were added to the vocabulary, both inside the "at least" list rather than against it.** `RefusalReason` gained **`missing-work`**: a work interval that is not on the day being changed has no honest sentence among the existing twelve (`missing-goal` is nonsense for a work block, `overlaps` is a lie), and a stale grid drop is a real event because the grid is where ids come from. `types.test.ts` was updated so the closed set still cannot grow quietly, and the PRD's domain model and prose list both now say so. `types.ts` also gained **`ResizeEdge` (`'start' | 'end'`)** — a resize reports one edge, which is what lets a drag and a typed field share a mutation, and it lives in `types.ts` because a dumb component has to be able to name it while the plan only lets components import types, `colors/palette.ts`, and display helpers. Task 10 will reuse it for sessions and one-offs.
- **Storage shape, also decided here.** Intervals are stored in **clock order** on every mutation, so a backup text reads the way the grid does and two intervals can never tie on a start. The day that changed is rebuilt down to fresh interval objects, so nothing on the returned board can be reached through the board it was made from; the other six days are shared, since nothing about them changed. `addWorkInterval` spends its id even when the change is refused — the check does not depend on it, it costs one uuid, and it keeps the function a single pass through the door. The test for ids passes one counter to both the preset and the add, which is the point: a second counter would hand Monday an id it already has.
- **`src/domain/work.test.ts`** (new, 71 tests) covers the done-when list and then the surrounding rules. The four product examples are their own `describe`, worked as the sequence of changes a person would make: leave at 12:00 by removing the afternoon; start earlier than 8:00 and not so early the commute cannot fit; an 8:00–10:00 appointment with the work at 10:00–12:00; a 9:30–10:30 appointment splitting the morning into 8:00–9:30 and 10:30–12:00 with the appointment proved to fit the hole through `checkFootprintFits`; and a weekend gaining and losing work. The commute is read back through `commuteFootprint` in every case, so a test asserts the geometry a person would see rather than the two numbers that made it. "The input board is unchanged" is a table: all five mutations against a `structuredClone` snapshot, plus a second table that the same holds for all six reasons work can refuse.

That is the union from task 7 doing its job, and the check is now in the helper with a comment saying so.

## Task 9 — Goal mutations [done]

- **`src/domain/goals.ts`** (new) is the task definitions: `createGoal`, `updateGoal`, `deleteGoal`, and `findGoal`. The lookup is exported rather than kept private because task 10 needs it — placing a session is where a block's first activity length and travel come from, so that is a reader, not a detail. No new refusal reason was needed: goals own six of the thirteen the board already has words for, and a test gathers every reason a real call can produce and asserts the set is exactly those six, so neither an invented reason nor a dead branch can slip in.
- **A draft is the whole goal, not a patch.** `GoalDraft` is a union on `kind` with the same discriminant the stored goal has (`TimeGoalDraft` / `CountGoalDraft`), which is what makes the kind lock cheap: `updateGoal` is handed a kind, and a draft whose kind does not match the goal it is editing is `kind-locked` rather than coerced. It also means a count goal cannot be handed minutes and a hue outside the twelve cannot be named — proved with a throwaway file: `goalMinutes` on a count draft fails with TS2353, `'fuchsia'` with TS2322, `kind: 'money'` with TS2322, and a draft missing `defaultTravel` with TS2345. The file was deleted after. A partial update would have needed `Partial` gymnastics across a discriminated union and would leave a name edit half applied; a form owns every field of a goal, so it submits all of them.
- **What an edit does not touch is the point.** A name and a color live on the goal and are never copied onto a session, so "changing the name changes every block of this task" is true by construction instead of by a loop that has to remember to visit every session — the test reads the label off the goal for every block of the task and for a block of another task. Only default travel is copied, and only to sessions whose `travelFollowsDefault` is still true. A name, color, quota, or default-length edit therefore rebuilds no session at all: not one object, and not the array either. That last part was a real finding, not a design flourish — the first version returned `sessions.map(...)`, so a name edit handed back an equal but new array while its own doc comment promised otherwise. `followingDefaultTravel` now tracks whether anything moved and hands the original array straight back when nothing did.
- **Create and update share one validator.** Both go through a private `draftRefusal`, so the two doors cannot disagree about what a legal goal is, and a table asserts ten bad drafts are refused identically through both. The field order is part of the rule rather than an accident of how the checks are written — name, then quota, then default block, then travel — because a form can be wrong several ways at once and only one sentence is shown. That was the one claim my probes caught me not having tested.
- **Four decisions are recorded in the module header and pinned by tests.** (1) *Quotas have their own rule and their own reason*: a visit count is a whole number of at least one, a time budget is at least one shortest activity, and neither is asked about the five-minute typed step, because the plan states that step for clock positions and a quota is a target rather than a time. A default **activity length** is the deliberate exception — it stops being a target and becomes a block's real minutes, so it is held to the step and 1:40 is legal. (2) *Names are stored exactly as typed*: a name of nothing but spaces is `empty-name`, since nobody meant to name a task that, but nothing is trimmed, because the app refuses rather than repairs. The 40-character limit is measured on the name that reaches the board, spaces included, which a probe forced me to state as its own test. (3) *Goals are appended and never sorted* — priorities are out of scope in the product scope, so list order is the order the person made them. (4) *A refused create mints no id*: the checks do not need one, so a refused change cannot spend the app's uuid source. This is a deliberate difference from `addWorkInterval`, which spends its id because it goes through one door in a single pass; there is no door here, so there is no reason to.
- **One rule was lifted rather than copied.** A commute and a task's default travel are the same rule from the plan — zero or at least five minutes, on the five-minute step, floor asked first — and `work.ts` already had it as a private `commuteRefusal`. Two copies of one rule is how a commute ends up accepting seven minutes while a task's default refuses it, so `checkTravelMinutes` now lives in `schedule.ts` beside the other length rules, and both `setCommute` and the default-travel check call it. `work.ts` lost its copy and its `MIN_TRAVEL_MINUTES` import; the reasoning about asking the floor first moved with the function. Task 8's pinned ordering test (three minutes `too-short`, seven `not-a-step`) passes unchanged, and a goals test asserts the same order for a default travel, so the shared rule is now covered from both sides.
- **`src/domain/goals.test.ts`** (new, 51 tests) works from a fixture board with two tasks and four placed blocks — a study block still following its default travel, a study block whose travel was edited for a different day and is already done, a gym visit whose duration the visit count does not care about, and a one-off — so every rule about where a field lives has something to be wrong about. Travel is read back through `sessionFootprint` rather than off the numbers, so a propagation test states the shape the grid would draw (`12:00–12:10 12:10–13:10 13:10–13:15`) and not just the two minutes that made it. `createGoal` is also pinned to *append*, to copy the draft's travel rather than hold the form's object, to mint no id when refused, and to leave the days, sessions, and one-offs shared by reference. Non-mutation is a table over five changes with a `structuredClone` snapshot, and the "shares nothing it rebuilt" companion names the goal each change rebuilt, since a create, an update, and a delete cannot be asserted with one loop.

The sixth was in the code, and is written up above.

Three tests were added for them, plus one for the field order, and the probes were re-run: 25 of 25. ## Task 10 — Session and one-off mutations [done]

- **`src/domain/sessions.ts`** (new) is everything a person can do to a block once it is on the board: `placeSession`, `moveSession`, `resizeSession`, `setSessionTravel`, `setSessionDone`, `deleteSession`, and the same six for one-offs. Twelve exports, none of which reaches for a date, a comparison, or an id generator of its own. Every fit check goes through one private `put` — `work.ts`'s `commit` again: build the block that would result, hand its footprint to `checkFootprintFits` excluding itself by id, and only then make a board. `checkFootprintFits` is what answers "does this fit in one free stretch", so no arithmetic about occupancy is written here at all.
- **A session and a one-off are told apart in exactly one function.** `isSession` asks whether the block has a `goalId`, which a `OneOff` cannot carry, so the narrowing is a real check rather than a guess. Everything else — the door, the footprint builder, the target, the resize — works on the shape the two share, which is what makes "the shortest activity is 15 minutes" mean one thing rather than two. A test drives the same four edits through the one-off door and asserts the sessions list never grows and the one-off never moves house, so the dispatch cannot quietly swap.
- **The signatures are the deliverable here, because tasks 17 and 18 call every one of them.** The three worth writing down:
  - `moveSession(board, id, day, startMinute)` takes the day as an **argument**. This is the one place a placed block and a work interval genuinely disagree, and it is deliberate: which days carry work is the day editor's question, while the product scope is explicit that moving a block from Monday to Friday moves it and leaves no copy, no ghost, and no missed mark. Nothing is re-checked against the day the block is leaving — that day simply loses it.
  - `resizeSession(board, id, edge, minute)` takes **one end of the activity**, not of the block. The plan's own `GridBlockView` settles this: travel is drawn as its own strip with its own `topPercent`, so the rectangle a finger lands on is the activity and the minute a drag reports is that rectangle's edge. The travel keeps its lengths and stays attached to the outside, so the activity is what grows or shrinks, and `activityMinutes` is the number task 11's progress will sum. The editor's fields are then the block's first minute (a move) and the activity length, both of which are numbers the caller already has, so no component and no orchestrator has to turn a length into an end position.
  - `setSessionTravel(board, id, travel)` is the only mutation that clears `travelFollowsDefault`, including when the person types the same numbers back. A move, a resize, and a done mark all leave the flag true, which is what lets a later edit of the task's default travel still reach the block.
- **A minute is asked first, then the task.** `placeSession` with a minute off the five-minute step and a task that does not exist says `not-a-step`, which is the sentence that is useful either way. That is the same order `work.ts` recorded and the same reason. The task is asked next because without one there is no length and no travel to place.
- **A resize must be asked about its length before its fit, and that ordering is load-bearing rather than tidy.** A start edge dragged past the activity's own end produces a negative `activityMinutes` and a footprint that `footprintRange` folds into a legal-looking sliver sitting exactly where the travel was — so the fit check on its own would *allow* it. `checkActivityMinutes` is asked first, on the number the resize would leave. The test is named for this.
- **The exclusion is by block id and never by task.** A block is not in its own way; a *second* visit of the same task on the same day is a different block and stays in the way. Two tests pin both halves: a move within a day is allowed, and a move of `s1` onto Tuesday where the other study block `s2` sits is refused `overlaps`.

## Task 11 — Progress sentences [done]

- **`src/domain/progress.ts`** (new) is the quota math and the sentence the list shows, in one module so the list cannot contradict itself. - **Decisions recorded in the module header and pinned by tests.**
  - **The short form counts hours itself rather than asking `formatDuration`.** Both sides whole hours reads `2 hours done of 5.` — the goal is left bare, because that is how the product scope's worked examples read, even though the long form names it. Anything less than whole on either side names both sides in full: `1 hour 40 minutes done of 5 hours.` Zero is a whole number of hours, so an empty task reads `0 hours done of 5.`
  - **This was a real bug the tests found, and the reason is worth keeping.** The first version rendered the done side of the short form with `formatDuration`, which spells a zero as `0 minutes` — correct for a length such as absent travel, wrong for a total. Every zero-done row came out as `0 minutes done of 5.`, naming two units in one clause. Task 4's own header had already flagged zero as "a length, not a silence", and the short form is the one place in the app that is counting hours rather than measuring something.
  - **The surplus clause is about the grid and waits until it is not redundant.** `2 hours done of 5. Nothing is unplaced. 1 hour more than the goal is on the grid.` is the product's second worked example; `6 hours done of 5. Nothing is unplaced.` is the third, and it is the same code with one clause absent, because once the done total is past the goal the first clause says so. So the clause is added only when the grid is over the goal and `done <= quota`. The boundary is pinned from both sides: `done` exactly equal to the goal still gets the clause, and one minute past it does not.
  - **Unplaced cannot go negative, and the surplus is measured on `placed`.** Since every done block is a placed block, `done <= placed` always, so measuring against the grid is the same as measuring against the done total and there is one surplus to keep. A test asserts `done <= placed` and `over === placed - goal` rather than only the wording.

  - **The verb agrees with the quantity, not with the presence of an hour.** `1 hour is`, `4 hours are`, and a quantity of minutes is plural however many hours are in it, so `1 hour 40 minutes are not on the grid.` and `1 hour 40 minutes more than the goal are on the grid.` `measure` reports the singular case as exactly one hour, or exactly one visit.

## Task 13 — Refusal copy [done]

- **`src/domain/refusal.ts`** (new) is the one place a reason is given words. One export, `refusalMessage(reason): string`, written as a switch over the whole closed set with **no default**, so a reason that joins the union fails the build here until it has a sentence (TS2366) — the same compile-time guarantee `types.test.ts` gives the closed set itself. The module imports one type and nothing else: `RefusalReason` from `@/domain/types`. - **The function takes the reason and nothing else, and that is the whole shape of the sentences.** A reason carries no block, no day, and no task, so the sentence cannot name one: the banner is shown at the moment of the refusal, where the person already knows what they were trying to do. Its only jobs are to say what is wrong and, where there is one, the next move — and the next move is one the app really offers, so `storage-unavailable` points at Backup and `missing-work` points at the day the work is actually on. A signature that also took the block would tempt every caller into wording its own case, which is the drift this module exists to end: an overlap refused by a drag and the same overlap refused by a typed field are the same sentence.
- **One sentence per reason, each honest everywhere its reason is produced.** That is the load-bearing rule: `too-short` comes back for an activity under fifteen minutes, for a work interval under fifteen, and for travel or commute under five, so its sentence names all three floors rather than the one the nearest check happens to produce — a sentence that named the caller would be a lie in the next module that returns the same reason. For the same reason `not-a-step` gives its example in bare minute numbers ("such as 35 or 40 rather than 37"), because the step applies to a typed clock time and a typed length alike and a clock example like 1:35 would be wrong for a length field; `work.ts`'s header uses the clock form for its own case, which is right there and wrong here.
- **Two sentences are written for producers that do not exist yet.** `invalid-backup` is task 15's and `storage-unavailable` is task 14's, and their sentences are written now from what the plan says about them — import validates then confirms and never repairs or merges, and a failed save keeps the in-memory board while Backup is the way to keep it — so those tasks arrive with their wording pinned rather than each inventing a voice. The closed set was fixed by task 3; the wording was owed now.
- **`src/domain/refusal.test.ts`** (new, 6 tests) pins the wording and the sentence-ness, in the order the plan asks for them: every reason gets one stable sentence **word for word** (a table, so wording cannot drift quietly); the table and the `REASONS` sweep list hold exactly the closed set; no sentence is empty; no two reasons share a sentence; every sentence ends with a full stop, like the progress sentences; and no hyphenated reason code reaches a person. The table is a **`Record<RefusalReason, string>`, so the compiler is the one that says a reason is missing from it** (TS2741) — the same total-map check `PALETTE` uses — while the runtime sweep is left to say what the wording is. The `REASONS` list itself is guarded only by the closed-set test, because the compiler cannot see a duplicate in a `RefusalReason[]`; that guard bites (probed).
- **One test bug was caught by running it, and it was in the test rather than the module.** The first version of the leak check asked each sentence not to contain its own reason code, and it caught my own sentence: `overlaps` is the one code that is also a plain English word, and using that word in its own sentence is what the sentence is for. The check is now against the hyphenated identifiers — unmistakably code, the thing a person should never read — across every sentence, so a probe that returns a raw identifier for any case is caught twice, by the table and by the leak sweep.
- **Nine wrong implementations were probed against the real files, and nine are caught.** Two reasons given the same sentence (2 failures); a sentence emptied (3); one word of wording drift (1); a case returning its raw reason code (3); a case swallowed by a `default` returning `No.` (1 — the table, which is what stops a swallowed case from showing a person a sentence nobody wrote); every full stop dropped (2); a case deleted outright, which fails the build with TS2366 rather than any test, proving the exhaustiveness is load-bearing; a new reason (`not-implemented`) joined to the union in `types.ts`, which fails the build in three places at once — the switch here (TS2366), the test's `Record` literal (TS2741), and `types.test.ts`'s own sweep (TS2366); and a duplicated entry in the test's `REASONS` list (2), which the compiler cannot see and the closed-set test is the only guard against. Every probe was reverted and both files diffed back identical against backups.

## Task 14 — Storage [done]

- **`src/persistence/storage.ts`** (new) is the one document the app keeps: what it opens with, and what it writes after every successful change. Exports: `STORAGE_KEY` (`weekly-planner.board`, pinned by a test against the literal), `BoardStorage` (`Pick<Storage, 'getItem' | 'setItem'>`), `isBoard`, `loadBoard(storage, createId)`, and `saveBoard(storage, board)`. The storage object is injected, so the app passes `window.localStorage` at the edge and tests pass a fake; neither function reads `window` itself. No new dependency, and `docs/product-scope.md` untouched.
- **`JSON.parse` lives in one private `parseStoredText(text): unknown`, and the hand-written `isBoard` guard is the only narrowing.** Nothing casts parsed text to a `Board` anywhere. The guard is exported because task 15's import reads pasted text through the same guard, so a board that loads and a board that imports are judged by one shape rather than two that can drift — the PRD says load fails on "a failed type guard" and import "runs the type guard", which is one guard, not two. Every guard branch is a named case in the test table (see below), so the guard and the table read like the same list.
- **`loadBoard` takes a `createId` alongside the storage object.** The plan writes `loadBoard(storage)`, but the preset is a function of `createId` (task 5), and the preset is exactly what load's failure modes hand back — a missing key, a storage that cannot be read from, text that does not parse, and text that is not a board. The round-trip test passes an id source that throws, which proves a good blob builds no preset at all.
- **Load never writes, on any path.** All four failure modes hand back the preset, leave the stored text exactly as it was (asserted against a seeded corrupt string), and record no `setItem` call; the happy path records none either. That is the plan's "do not write yet" and "do not overwrite the stored text on load", and the known limit "a bad blob is not destroyed on load" — the next successful save is what replaces it.
- **`saveBoard` returns `Result<null>`.** A save has no product: the caller already holds the board, and the only fact worth reporting is whether it landed. `null` says that without inviting a caller to read a value back that was never made — an orchestrator cannot write `setBoard(saveResult.value)` if there is nothing to read. A failed write refuses with `storage-unavailable` instead of throwing, which is task 13's pinned sentence ("Keep a copy with Backup.") finding its producer.
- **The guard checks shape and closed sets only, and that line is load-bearing.** It checks the version, all seven days (`DAYS`), all twelve colors (`COLOR_IDS` from `@/colors/palette`, the one source of the list — membership must be checked, because a `colorId` outside the twelve would make `PALETTE[colorId].solid` throw inside a component), the seven weekdays, strings, booleans, and finite numbers. It deliberately does not check that a session's task exists or that nothing overlaps: those are the invariants a live edit is refused for, task 15's import runs them after this guard, and a stored board that breaks them is drawn by the view models as it is rather than hidden. The direct `isBoard` test adds Infinity and NaN cases, because JSON cannot spell either (`1e400` parses to `Infinity`, and `JSON.stringify(Infinity)` serializes to `null`) — the "finite" check is unreachable through text, so it is tested on the object the guard actually takes.
- **Two more decisions, recorded in the module header and pinned by tests.** *Save writes one line of JSON*: the two-space pretty form is the Backup export, which a person reads in a text area; storage is not read by a person, so it carries no indentation. *An unreadable storage reads as "no board here"*: `getItem` throwing is handled like a missing key, because `loadBoard` must return a board and the preset is the board with nothing on it; only `setItem` throwing is a refusal, because that is the one the person can act on.
- **`src/persistence/storage.test.ts`** (new, 30 tests) covers the four done-when cases and then the guard's whole branch list. The round trip saves a **realWeek** built through the real mutations — a time task and a count task, a block whose travel was edited (so `travelFollowsDefault: false` is on the wire), a done block, a done one-off, weekend work, and a commute — so it proves the document the app actually writes comes back, not a hand-typed shape that happens to match. Nineteen broken-blob cases are one field of that same week broken at a time (null, a bare string, an empty object, an array, version 2, a missing Monday, work that is not a list, a word minute, a word commute, a fuchsia color, a missing quota, a `money` kind, a word count, a made-up day, a missing `goalId`, a missing `travelFollowsDefault` flag, a word done mark, a `null` minute, one-sided travel, a nameless one-off), each asserting preset + stored text untouched + no write. The surgery is done on `Record<string, unknown>` values through a local type predicate, never a cast — the same rule the module itself follows.
- **Fifteen wrong implementations were probed against the real file, and all fifteen are caught.** Save rethrowing instead of refusing; save returning ok without writing; the pretty two-space form; a handed-back board as the value; another key; load writing the preset on a missing key; load overwriting corrupt text; a cast instead of the guard; an unreadable storage left to throw; and the guard dropping the version, the seven days, color membership, the finite check, the kind discriminant, and the `travelFollowsDefault` flag. Every probe was reverted and the file diffed back identical against a backup.
